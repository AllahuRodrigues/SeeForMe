This page is the working answer to "what to do now?" after the EgoBlind and CampusBench v2 results. It is
a plan with status, not a result; numbers live in `docs/egoblind.md` and `docs/research_v2.md`.

## The situation in three lines

1. On synthetic scenes (CampusBench v2) the learned scheduler beats the linear P-CACS rules and the
   chatty baselines; with oracle perception it reaches 0.70 timely recall against the reference's 0.74.
2. On real blind-user video (EgoBlind) everything tuned or trained on the question-derived labels goes
   silent or nearly so, and the recall diagnosis says why: 46% of what users asked about is outside the
   detector's vocabulary, 9% was never detected, and of the rest most score far below the speak threshold
   because the labels reward *awareness* (a person nearby) where P-CACS models *collision* (a person on a
   course). Neither a lower threshold nor a proximity term fixes that; an open-vocabulary detector widens the
   vocabulary but still misses doors and stairs; training the imitation policy on real video only is the one
   change that moved recall (0.23 of coverable validation events at one cue a minute).
3. The product story (proactive, timed, budgeted cues on glasses) is credible only once real-video
   recall is non-trivial with a human-verified event set; until then every claim is simulator-only.

## Workstream decisions

* **Continue EgoBlind** with the frozen splits (val = train videos with `video_id % 7 == 0`). Add the
  test split only when its zips download; never tune on it.
* **Keep CampusBench v2** as the controlled instrument. Its job is mechanism studies (contention, memory,
  encoders, stress), not headline numbers.
* **Defer** JRDB, DADA-2000 and any foundation-model fine-tuning: none of them answers the question the
  diagnosis raised (vocabulary and label semantics), and the 7B VLM route needs GPUs this project does not have.
* **Glasses prototype separate**: an app-engineering track that consumes the frozen scheduler; it must not
  change the research code.

## Positioning (strategy notes, not results)

Be My Eyes and similar services are *reactive*: the user asks, a volunteer or a VLM answers. SeeForMe's claim
is *proactive and rationed*: it decides when silence is better than speech, speaks before the user would
have had to ask, and interrupts only for a more severe hazard. That claim is measurable (timely recall,
quiet-window violations, airtime) and the EgoBlind windows are the first real-world test of it. A beta,
an SDK for other assistive apps, or an exit all rest on the same artefact: a scheduler whose decisions
are reproducible from a logged feature vector and whose behaviour under stated assumptions is verifiable
(the Koenig item in `docs/research_v2.md`). Until the human-verified real-video numbers exist, the honest
pitch is the method and the benchmark, not safety.
