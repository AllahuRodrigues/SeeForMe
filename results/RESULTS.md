# SeeForMe results (2026-09-24)

Generated by `scripts/write_results_md.py` from the files in `results/`. Every number below is reproducible with `make all && make latency && make report`.

## 1. Setup

* **Benchmark.** Synthetic scenarios from `seeforme/sim/scenario.py`: six scene types (hallway, office, street, cluttered room, low light, text corridor), 30 s each at 10 detections/s, with a noisy detector model (missed detections that depend on object size, confidence noise, label confusions, OCR character corruptions, clutter false positives, low-light/blur degradation). Every object carries ground-truth *important windows*.
* **Splits.** train = 36 scenarios (used to tune CACS weights and train the learned models), val = 18 (model selection and learned-model thresholds), test = 36 (all numbers below). Each test scenario is run with 3 detector-noise seeds, so each policy is scored on 108 runs covering 108 × ~5 = ~550 important events.
* **Audio channel.** All policies share the same channel model: one message at a time, occupying the channel for max(1.2 s, 0.35 s × words). Decisions every 0.5 s.
* **Metrics** (section 5 of the proposal): important-cue recall, cue precision (a cue is useful if its object is inside an important window and was not announced in the previous 6 s), audio load (cues/min), duplicate rate, critical-event delay (s from window start to first announcement).

**Answer to the research question.** CACS speaks 8.6 cues/min instead of 35.9 (confidence threshold) or 35.2 (confidence + cooldown), a 75% reduction in audio load, while *raising* important-cue recall from 0.21 / 0.44 to 0.82 and cue precision from 0.06 / 0.13 to 0.94. Duplicate rate falls from 0.21 to 0.002. Critical-event delay is also lower (1.77 s vs 2.75 s) because the baselines waste the audio channel on background objects while an obstacle approaches.

The baselines are not straw men: baseline 1 cycles through every confident detection (a queue, so nothing is skipped) and baseline 2 adds a 5 s per-label cooldown. Both keep the channel saturated with high-confidence background objects (clock, tv, cell phone, decorative signs) because model confidence is not user importance, exactly the failure mode described in section 3 of the proposal. No policy ever spoke a clutter false positive (all 0), because the tracker's two-hit confirmation filters transient detections before any policy sees them.

`results/demo_hallway_*.mp4` show the same hallway scenario under each policy: the confidence baseline says "clock / tv / cell phone / person" 19 times in 30 s; CACS speaks 5 times, each about an obstacle entering the walking path or the STAIRS sign.

## 3. Manual vs learned weighting

**Tuned linear CACS** (random search on the train split, objective = F1 of recall and precision, 150 trials): α(confidence)=0.05, β(relevance)=0.23, γ(novelty)=1.59, δ(proximity)=1.55, η(text)=1.80, λ(duplicate)=0.77, threshold=2.78, duplicate time constant=6 s. Train-split F1 rose from 0.605 (hand-set defaults) to 0.812.

**Learned models** are trained on 12,244 (observation, useful-now) pairs from the train split (positive rate 1.8%) and validated on 6,464 from val:

| model | val AUC | val average precision | val F1 at chosen threshold | threshold (chosen on val) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| logreg | 0.997 | 0.787 | 0.854 | 0.92 |
| gbm | 0.997 | 0.836 | 0.792 | 0.46 |
| mlp_torch | 0.998 | 0.889 | 0.858 | 0.98 |

Logistic-regression coefficients (standardised inputs not used, so these are on the raw [0,1] features): confidence=+0.5, relevance=+4.4, novelty=+8.5, proximity=+16.4, text_importance=+10.6, duplicate=-4.6. The learned ordering, proximity > text importance > novelty > |duplicate| > relevance > confidence, matches the tuned manual weights: **confidence is the least useful signal for deciding what to say**, which is the proposal's central claim.

These first learned variants (`cacs_learned:*`) were trained *off-policy*: their announcement history came from a noisy oracle, not from their own closed-loop behaviour, and their thresholds were chosen for single-decision F1 rather than for the closed-loop trade-off. Section 3.1 fixes both.

### 3.1 On-policy training (DAgger) and the deep context model

All models in this section are trained on 16,362 oracle-labelled candidate observations (positive rate 3.2%) and then improved with DAgger: the current model is run in closed loop on the train split, the states it actually visits are labelled by the oracle, aggregated, and the model is retrained. Thresholds are selected by *closed-loop* F1 on the validation split. The deep context model is a GRU over the last 8 decision steps of each track (13 signals per step: the six CACS features plus position, size, growth rate, age, time since announcement, announcement count), trained first by imitation and then fine-tuned with REINFORCE (reward +1 useful cue, −0.5 useless cue, −0.75 duplicate, −0.25 for staying silent while a useful candidate is visible; the audio channel is part of the environment).

### 3.2 Interpretation

| policy | F1 (recall, precision) | recall | precision | cues/min | critical delay (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cacs | 0.877 | 0.821 | 0.941 | 8.6 | 1.77 |
| cacs_learned:logreg | 0.674 | 0.715 | 0.637 | 10.9 | 0.98 |
| cacs_learned:gbm | 0.745 | 0.754 | 0.736 | 10.4 | 0.79 |
| cacs_learned:mlp_torch | 0.729 | 0.731 | 0.728 | 10.2 | 0.71 |
| cacs_dagger:logreg | 0.738 | 0.756 | 0.722 | 10.6 | 0.91 |
| cacs_dagger:gbm | 0.815 | 0.805 | 0.825 | 10.1 | 0.98 |
| cacs_dagger:mlp13 | 0.782 | 0.779 | 0.785 | 10.1 | 0.70 |
| cacs_seq_il | 0.744 | 0.752 | 0.735 | 10.4 | 0.82 |
| cacs_seq_rl | 0.853 | 0.853 | 0.854 | 10.2 | 0.85 |

* **DAgger works.** Training on the states a policy actually visits improves every feature model over its off-policy twin: logreg recall +0.04 / precision +0.08, GBM +0.05 / +0.09, MLP +0.05 / +0.06 (the MLP also gains the seven extra inputs).
* **Reward fine-tuning is what makes the sequence model competitive.** Imitation alone leaves the GRU at recall 0.75 / precision 0.74; REINFORCE in closed loop lifts it to 0.85 / 0.85, the highest recall of any policy, with a critical-event delay of 0.85 s against 1.77 s for the linear rule: it learns to speak *while an object is still approaching* instead of waiting for the proximity term to grow.
* **The tuned linear rule is still the best single operating point** by F1 (0.877 vs 0.853 for `cacs_seq_rl`), and it is the quietest and most precise policy. The learned policies sit at a different point of the trade-off (about 1.5 more cues/min, 0.09 lower precision, faster reaction). Their validation F1 was higher than their test F1, so part of the gap is model/threshold selection on only 18 validation scenarios: the honest reading is that learned weighting matches manual weighting on this benchmark rather than beating it, and that the recall/latency gains are real while the precision cost is real too.
* **What this means for the thesis question.** Both the hand-tuned rule and the learned policies agree on *what matters*: proximity and text importance first, novelty and duplicate memory next, confidence last. Manual vs learned weighting is a secondary question; the primary result, a 75% cut in audio load with higher important-cue recall than either baseline, holds for every CACS variant.

## 7. Limitations and next steps

1. **Synthetic benchmark.** Object trajectories, detector noise and importance windows are simulated. The numbers show the *relative* behaviour of the policies under identical perception noise; absolute values will differ on real video. `scripts/run_video.py` + `scripts/score_video.py` and `docs/annotation_format.md` are ready for hand-annotated recordings (planned for the weeks of Nov 2–9 in the proposal schedule).
2. **Ground truth is a proxy for user importance.** Important windows are defined geometrically (object enters the walking path, sign becomes legible). A study with blind and low-vision participants is out of scope for the semester, as stated in the proposal.
3. **Learned models.** Section 3.1 trains on-policy (DAgger) and adds a sequence model with REINFORCE fine-tuning; a vision-language model fine-tuned on cue selection (LLaVA / InternVL, as in the expanded plan) remains future work because it needs GPU time and real annotated video.
4. **Proximity is monocular.** A depth estimate (or bbox-height-to-distance using `TYPICAL_HEIGHT_M`) would fix the street failure case and make the proximity term physically meaningful.
